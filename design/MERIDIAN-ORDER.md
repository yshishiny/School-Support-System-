# Meridian Order

*A design philosophy.*

---

## I.

Meridian Order begins from a single conviction: **position is meaning, and colour is expensive.** An interface that spends colour freely has nothing left to say when something matters. Under this philosophy the ground is deep and quiet — an ink that reads as material rather than absence — and across it runs a measured structure of hairlines and intervals, the way a meridian runs across a chart of the sky. Everything sits where it sits *for a reason*, and the reason is legible without being announced. The discipline is subtractive. The work is finished not when there is nothing left to add, but when nothing further can be removed without the structure collapsing.

## II. Space and form

Form here is **ruled, not drawn**. The page is governed by an armature of thin lines — a ledger, a staff, a set of coordinates — and every element either sits on that armature or deliberately breaks it, which is how emphasis is made. Generous margins are not empty; they are the silence that lets a single mark carry weight. Rectangles are exact. Circles are true. Arcs are struck from a real centre with a real radius, never approximated, because a master's hand is visible precisely in the places an amateur would round off. Density is permitted, even welcomed, but only as *accumulation* — many identical small marks building a field — never as clutter. The eye should be able to rest anywhere on the composition and find the spacing correct.

## III. Colour and material

One accent. **One.** A warm metal against the cold ground — brass, ochre, the colour of low sun on stone — and it appears perhaps four times on a page, each appearance earned. Everything else is the ground and three or four calibrated greys stepping between them, each step deliberate, tested, and held to across the entire system. Where a second hue is unavoidable it must be cold and quiet, a counterweight rather than a rival. Material is suggested through restraint: the faint tooth of paper, the slight weight of an inked rule, the sense that a hand pressed here. Gradients are suspect. Shadow is nearly forbidden. Depth comes from value and overlap, not from imitation glass. This chromatic economy must feel like the product of painstaking calibration — dozens of variants tried and discarded — because it is.

## IV. Scale and rhythm

Rhythm is the philosophy's true subject. A composition under Meridian Order carries a **pulse**: a repeating interval — ticks, rules, modules, degrees — against which larger forms are heard as syncopation. Establish the beat with patient repetition, then break it exactly once, and that break becomes the whole meaning of the page. Scale is extreme and intentional: one element enormous and calm, everything else small and precise, with almost nothing in the mediocre middle. Type obeys the same law. A single display gesture, set thin and wide and treated as *drawing* rather than as writing, against clinical monospaced annotation so small it reads as texture until approached. Never paragraphs. Labels, coordinates, a date, a plate number. Words are landmarks, not explanation.

## V. Composition and hierarchy

Hierarchy is achieved through **position, interval and scarcity** — never through decoration. What matters most is either the largest thing or the only coloured thing, and never both at once. The composition should reward two distances: from across a room it resolves into one clear form; at arm's length it reveals a second layer of systematic detail that was invisible before — small numerals, registration marks, the quiet apparatus of measurement. This double reading is the signature of work that has been laboured over. It cannot be faked and it cannot be rushed. Alignment is absolute: every edge answers another edge, every gap is a multiple of the module, and nothing — *nothing* — overlaps, crops or crowds. Margins are inviolable.

## VI. The standard

The finished artefact must carry the unmistakable evidence of expertise: the sense of countless hours, of a practitioner at the very top of their field who cared about the third decimal place of a position nobody will consciously notice. Every rule weight chosen against alternatives. Every interval measured. Every label placed, moved, and placed again. The result should read as an instrument rather than an illustration — something made to be *consulted*, precise enough to be trusted, restrained enough to be lived with for years. Meticulously crafted, and quiet about it. That quietness is the point: mastery announcing itself is not mastery.

---

*Meridian Order does not decorate information. It gives information a place to stand.*
